Tool-using agents are increasingly trained to predict more than their next action: the observations the environment will return, the outcome of a call, or the answer they are looking for, and such predictions are credited with making later reinforcement learning (RL) more effective. Between learning a prediction and acting on it, however, lie three separate steps. The knowledge must be acquired, it must enter the context in which actions are chosen, and the decision rule must use it. Know2Act (from \emph{knowledge to action}) is a pre-registered study of a multi-turn text-to-SQL agent, trained with supervised fine-tuning and then GRPO, that measures each step on its own. Every prediction we supervise is learned and transfers to unseen databases, from the structure of result tables to an answer contract that states the shape of the correct answer. Whether a prediction reaches the action policy depends on where it is placed: the same outcome line moves the policy only when it is written into the action. The learned contract is usable at once from outside the agent, as a selector among sampled answers that adds to execution-based voting. Yet the agent's own decision to submit or keep querying does not consult it: holding the model and context fixed, swapping or removing the contract at the decision point changes the check the agent writes next but not whether it submits. RL, which rewards only the final answer, gives no variant an advantage and removes even the selector's gain. Learned knowledge thus reaches selection before it reaches decisions, and each step needs its own test. We also find that 44\% of the training tasks in a public SQL RL environment reward an empty answer.
