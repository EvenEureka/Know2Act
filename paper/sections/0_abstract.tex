\begin{abstract}
Agents are often trained to write a prediction before acting, which people read to monitor or steer the agents. We ask whether such a prediction controls the next action when the knowledge it states is also in the weights, and how the fine-tuning data decide this. In a counterfactual ALFWorld whose object locations only fine-tuning can teach, Qwen3-4B agents write a one-line location prediction before each search. When training predictions are always followed, deleting the prediction at the first search step lowers the predicted move's probability by 0.015. Replacing it with the agent's prediction for another object redirects 80\% of first steps. A randomly ordered ranking, never seen in training, redirects even more (90\%), and, in held-out rooms, objects whose location the weights cannot know are redirected just as often. When a quarter or half of the training predictions are contradicted by the action, redirection falls to 1.2\% and 0.3\%, as a perfect imitator of those data would. In ALF-CF, when the training data leave open whether the action follows the prediction or the learned convention, fine-tuning produces agents that follow the prediction; contradicting a quarter of the training predictions removes this preference while the agents keep writing predictions.
\end{abstract}
