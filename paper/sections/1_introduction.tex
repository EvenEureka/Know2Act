\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{The proposal we test.}
A tool-using agent's trajectory interleaves decisions with their consequences. The agent issues a query, the environment returns a result, and the agent decides what to do next. Standard supervised fine-tuning (SFT) learns from one side of this exchange. The loss covers the agent's actions, while observations stay in the context but are not prediction targets.

\citet{zhang2026actobs} propose removing this mask. Their method, ActObs, also supervises the observation tokens. It reports that, although the SFT checkpoints perform alike, GRPO from ActObs reaches higher pass@$k$ on Terminal-Bench. The authors attribute this to the policy learning what its actions do to the environment. Related work adds observation losses during RL \citep{shrivastava2026echo,lu2026paw}.

\paragraph{Three steps between a prediction and an action.}
For a predictive objective to improve what an agent does, three things must each hold, and a reported gain does not say which of them does. The knowledge must be \emph{acquired}: the model must predict its target better on data it has not seen. It must \emph{enter the context in which actions are chosen}: a prediction the model can make but never conditions on cannot change what it writes next. And the decision rule must \emph{use} it: an agent that knows what a correct answer looks like should submit when its result has that shape and keep working when it does not. Two explanations of a gain involve no knowledge at all: a pooled loss over actions and observations shrinks the share of each update that goes to actions, and less action fitting could preserve plasticity for RL by itself \citep{plasticity2026}, while a pre-registered study found that content-free auxiliary losses match real observation losses \citep{wang2026darkroom}. A last question is specific to tools that are cheap to call, such as an SQL engine: the agent can always run its query and see the result, so which knowledge about such an environment is still worth learning?

\paragraph{Our study.}
We test these questions in Know2Act (from \emph{knowledge to action}), a study of a multi-turn text-to-SQL agent that writes SQL, runs it, reads the table or error, and answers \citep{skyrl} (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). We use a Qwen3-4B student, GRPO, and the Spider and BIRD development sets. The study was pre-registered before any main run. It is token-faithful, in that SFT sequences match, token for token, the stream that RL trains on, and it includes controls that reproduce the observation-supervised model's action share and action movement. Beyond observation supervision we vary what the agent predicts and where: an outcome line placed outside or inside the action, and an answer contract that states the shape of the correct answer and is checked against every result. For each variant we measure the three steps separately: prediction quality on unseen databases, action movement calibrated against a data-order noise floor, and the sensitivity of the submit-or-continue decision to whether a result has the right shape.

\paragraph{What we find.}
The knowledge is acquired: every target is learned and transfers to databases that SFT never saw, and the contract states the correct answer shape for 89\% and 82\% of the questions on the two benchmarks. Whether it enters the actions depends on placement: the same outcome line, learned equally well in both positions, moves the action policy only once it is part of the action, while observation supervision, whose targets are never generated at test time, moves the policy less than retraining on a reshuffled data order does. The knowledge is already usable from outside the agent: before RL, selecting among sampled answers by the contract beats an execution-validity filter and adds to majority voting over execution results. The agent's own decision, however, does not use it. No variant changes how much more often the agent submits after a result with the right shape than after one without, beyond what a reshuffled data order changes. A decision-point intervention makes this causal: when the contract in the agent's own prefix is flipped or removed, the check it writes next follows the new contract, but the probability that it submits moves by less than 0.01. RL, which rewards only the final answer, then gives no variant an advantage, removes the selector's gain, and makes every variant more willing to submit after a failed check.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
Our contributions are threefold. (1)~We separate the acquisition of knowledge, its entry into the action context, and its use in decisions, and give each step a measurement calibrated against training noise: prediction quality on unseen databases, action KL against a data-order noise floor, and decision sensitivity against a data-order or RL-seed reference. (2)~A pre-registered, token-faithful test of observation supervision for a multi-turn SQL agent with matched controls finds that the knowledge is learned, as transferable structure and memorised content, but stops before the action policy, and that RL gains nothing from it. Our audit of the public RL environment also finds that 44\% of its training tasks reward an empty answer. (3)~Across five supervision targets and two placements, placement decides whether a prediction moves the policy, the learned answer contract improves inference-time selection, and no variant changes submit-or-revise decisions beyond retraining noise; holding model and context fixed, the decision ignores the contract and the agent's own check, although both are read.
