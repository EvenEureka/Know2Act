\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Supervising a tool-using agent on its observations teaches it their structure and the training databases' contents. It does not teach anything the agent uses when acting: the action policy is unchanged, behaviour after errors is unchanged, and RL gains nothing.

We then changed what the agent predicts and where. Placement matters for the action tokens: the same outcome line moves the policy only once it is part of the action. It does not matter for decisions. Even the shape of the correct answer is learned on unseen databases, written into the action stream and checked against every observation. Still, the agent submits after a mismatching result as often as an action-only model does. RL makes all variants submit more readily, and the contract then stops helping even as an external selector.

Learning about the environment, or even about the answer, is therefore not enough. Decisions follow imitation and the outcome reward. Making knowledge reach them probably requires rewarding its use, or training on the agent's own failure-rich interaction.
